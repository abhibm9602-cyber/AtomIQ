# Machine Learning in Materials Science

## ML for Materials Discovery
- Paradigm shift from trial-and-error experimental synthesis and computationally expensive ab initio calculations to data-driven prediction.
- High-throughput virtual screening: evaluating thousands of hypothetical crystal structures using fast ML surrogate models to identify promising candidates.
- Inverse design: specifying desired target properties (e.g., band gap = 1.5 eV, high stability) and using generative ML to output the corresponding atomic structures or compositions.
- Accelerates the discovery of novel battery cathodes, solid-state electrolytes, thermoelectric materials, and catalysts.

## Descriptors and Features
- Raw Cartesian coordinates are not rotationally, translationally, or permutationally invariant. Descriptors map atomic environments to invariant feature vectors.
- Compositional features: based on elemental properties (e.g., electronegativity, atomic radius, valence electrons) computed from the stoichiometric formula (e.g., Magpie features).
- Coulomb Matrix: M_ij = Z_i Z_j / |R_i - R_j| for i!=j, M_ii = 0.5 Z_i^2.4. Global descriptor, requires padding for varying molecule sizes.
- ACSF (Atom-Centered Symmetry Functions): local radial and angular basis functions capturing the local atomic environment.
- SOAP (Smooth Overlap of Atomic Positions): expands the local atomic density into spherical harmonics and spherical Bessel functions, forming a rotationally invariant power spectrum. Provides rigorous geometric completeness.
- ACE (Atomic Cluster Expansion): generalized framework for systematically complete linear basis sets for local environments.

## Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) for Crystals
- Crystals naturally map to graphs: atoms are nodes (features = atomic number, embedding), bonds/distances are edges.
- Message Passing Neural Networks (MPNNs): nodes iteratively update their hidden state by aggregating messages from neighbors.
- CGCNN (Crystal Graph Convolutional Neural Network): early architecture using bond distance embeddings and node feature updates.
- MEGNet (Materials Graph Network): incorporates global state variables (e.g., temperature, pressure) along with node and edge features.
- SchNet: uses continuous-filter convolutional layers, modeling many-body interactions based on distances without explicitly defining bonds.
- DimeNet, GemNet: incorporate directional message passing (bond angles, dihedral angles) for higher accuracy, distinguishing structures that distance-only models cannot.
- MACE, NequIP: E(3)-equivariant GNNs that preserve the rotational symmetries of physics tensors, operating on spherical harmonic representations. State-of-the-art accuracy for potentials.

## Property Prediction
- Training datasets derived from high-throughput DFT calculations (e.g., Materials Project, OQMD, AFLOW).
- Target properties: Formation energy (E_form), band gap (E_g), bulk/shear moduli, Debye temperature, thermal conductivity, magnetic moments.
- Classification tasks: Metal vs. non-metal, topologically trivial vs. non-trivial, stable vs. unstable (distance to the convex hull, E_hull).
- Model evaluation: Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), R^2 coefficient.
- Matbench: a curated suite of diverse benchmark datasets for evaluating ML models on materials science tasks.
- OCP (Open Catalyst Project) OC20/OC22 datasets: massive datasets for training models to predict adsorption energies and forces for catalyst design.

## Active Learning and Uncertainty
- Strategy to efficiently sample the vast chemical space by only labeling (calculating via DFT) the most informative data points.
- Uncertainty quantification (UQ) is critical: the model must "know what it doesn't know".
- Ensemble methods / Query-by-committee: training multiple models on subsets of data. The variance of their predictions estimates epistemic uncertainty.
- Gaussian Processes (GPs) and Bayesian Neural Networks naturally provide posterior predictive distributions (mean and variance).
- Acquisition functions (e.g., Expected Improvement, Upper Confidence Bound) guide the selection of new candidates to simultaneously explore high-uncertainty regions and exploit known high-performing regions (Bayesian Optimization).

## Generative Models for Crystal Structure
- Goal: generate valid, stable, and novel crystal structures (atomic species + lattice vectors + fractional coordinates).
- VAEs (Variational Autoencoders): compress structures into a continuous latent space. Decoding interpolations in latent space yields new materials. CDVAE (Crystal Diffusion VAE) combines VAEs with diffusion.
- GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks): a generator creates structures to fool a discriminator trained on real crystals.
- Flow-matching and Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM): iteratively add noise to coordinates/lattice, then learn to reverse the noising process to generate structures from pure noise. E.g., MatterGen, UniMat.

## Pre-training and Foundational Models
- Transfer learning: pre-training a GNN on millions of structures for self-supervised tasks (e.g., predicting masked atoms, denoising coordinates), then fine-tuning on a small dataset for a specific property.
- "Universal" Interatomic Potentials: foundational models trained on nearly all available DFT data across the periodic table.
- M3GNet, CHGNet (Charge-Informed Graph Neural Network), MACE-MP-0.
- These models act as out-of-the-box surrogate DFT engines, capable of relaxing arbitrary crystal structures, calculating phonon spectra, and running MD for completely unseen materials with high reliability.

## Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)
- Embedding known physical laws (differential equations, thermodynamics) directly into the neural network architecture or loss function.
- Loss = L_data + lambda * L_physics.
- Examples: enforcing energy conservation, exact thermodynamic relations, or satisfying the Navier-Stokes equations for fluid flow. Ensures predictions are physically consistent even outside the training domain.

## RAG for Materials Science
- Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) utilizes Large Language Models (LLMs) combined with vector databases of scientific literature.
- Addresses the unstructured data problem: synthesizing synthesis recipes, extraction of experimental property data (T_c, yield strength) buried in PDF text and tables.
- Systems like MatSciBERT or specialized Llama models embed queries and retrieve relevant paragraphs from millions of papers, grounding the LLM's response in factual literature to prevent hallucination.
- Automating literature reviews, extracting reaction conditions, and building structured knowledge bases (like this one) from raw scientific text.
